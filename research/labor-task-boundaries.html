Reshaping Labor Task Boundaries after AI Adoption

Abstract

This paper studies how organizations allocate AI-adopted tasks between augmentation and automation, and why this allocation differs across task structures. Rather than treating the labor effect of AI as a single aggregate outcome, the paper models the augmentation-automation boundary as an endogenous organizational choice within an AI-adopted workflow. I develop a continuous task-chain model in which task-chain continuity, judgment non-substitutability, and new-task creation potential jointly determine the boundary. The model shows that stronger task-chain continuity expands the automation interval, while stronger judgment non-substitutability and greater new-task creation potential expand the augmentation interval. New-task creation also raises conditional labor input within the AI-adopted task domain. The main theoretical result is a reversal in the labor effect of AI capability improvements: when capability gains mainly reduce the relative cost of automation, conditional labor input falls; when capability gains more strongly increase new-task returns, conditional labor input rises. After new-task creation is endogenized, this reversal also depends on organizational adjustment costs. The paper therefore does not claim to explain aggregate employment changes directly. It provides a task-structure mechanism for heterogeneous labor effects across industries, occupations, and workflows. The appendices report formal proofs, numerical mechanism displays, a finite task-chain extension, and an exploratory measurement exercise.
